# Scientific Synthesis

Status: `complete / LOSS_PRIORITY_SIGNAL`

Evidence mode: `Ran`

## Result

The frozen cohort screen selects `LOSS_PRIORITY_SIGNAL`. Three of four sites
(Niwot, Paradise, and Snowbird) pass the dry-interval excess-loss screen, while
only Snowbird passes the current-input mass-ceiling screen. Neither all-phase
input nor modeled snowfall is systematically short during the selected cold
observed-accumulation events.

This is a prioritization result, not a single-cause attribution. It says the
next cohort-wide physics investigation should concentrate on pre-peak loss,
especially warm or mixed dry-period melt. It does not clear the forcing or
observation operators, and it does not authorize a correction.

## Seasonal Feasibility

The zero-loss ceiling asks a deliberately generous question: could initial SWE
plus every millimeter of fixture precipitation through the observed peak equal
the observed pillow peak, even if all rain were retained and no mass were lost?

| Site | Complete years | Median ceiling / observed peak | Years below 1 | Screen |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Mica Creek | 34 | 1.539 | 0.0% | no input ceiling |
| Niwot | 44 | 1.198 | 4.5% | no input ceiling |
| Paradise | 41 | 1.105 | 9.8% | no input ceiling |
| Snowbird | 35 | 0.823 | 97.1% | input ceiling |

Snowbird is therefore mass-limited under the current no-lateral-input fixture:
loss-free retention still reaches only 82.3% of the observed peak in the median
year. That prevents an all-loss explanation at Snowbird. The other three sites
usually have enough all-phase fixture input in the gross seasonal ledger, but
their modeled observed-date storage remains only 54.4%, 29.9%, and 41.6% of the
observed peak, respectively.

## Cold Accumulation Events

Across 1,217 prospectively selected events, every site has median all-phase and
modeled-snowfall ratios above one:

| Site | Events | All phase / observed gain | Snowfall / observed gain | Storage change / observed gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mica Creek | 234 | 1.660 | 1.463 | 1.384 |
| Niwot | 383 | 1.323 | 1.299 | 1.204 |
| Paradise | 391 | 1.239 | 1.110 | 1.056 |
| Snowbird | 209 | 1.234 | 1.167 | 1.124 |

The selected cold events do not support a systemic gross-input or
snowfall-partition shortfall. This is narrower than saying the forcing is
correct. The event denominator sums only active-day WTEQ gains of at least
5.08 mm, while input and modeled terms include the prospectively frozen
one-day timing padding. This timing-tolerant upper-alignment diagnostic can
mask smaller or padding-day observed gains and cannot resolve gauge catch,
canopy, elevation, footprint, or subdaily phase errors.

An independent active-day-only sensitivity check shows that this qualification
is consequential:

|---|---:|---:|
| Mica Creek | 0.943 / 0.218 | 0.914 / 0.265 |
| Niwot | 0.873 / 0.379 | 0.869 / 0.379 |
| Paradise | 0.719 / 0.652 | 0.699 / 0.691 |
| Snowbird | 0.756 / 0.603 | 0.754 / 0.603 |

Paradise and Snowbird pass both site-level event screens without padding, but
two of four remains below the frozen three-site systemic rule. The robust claim
is therefore only that gross cold-event input shortfall is not systemic under
either alignment; the zero-site result belongs specifically to the frozen
timing-tolerant operator.

## Guarded Dry-Interval Loss

Annual-first modeled-minus-observed dry loss passes the frozen screen at three
sites:

| Site | Annuals | Median observed loss (m) | Median modeled loss (m) | Median difference (m) | Positive years | Screen |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Mica Creek | 18 | 0.000 | 0.011 | 0.005 | 61.1% | no |
| Niwot | 34 | 0.006 | 0.041 | 0.034 | 85.3% | yes |
| Paradise | 29 | 0.000 | 0.086 | 0.086 | 93.1% | yes |
| Snowbird | 10 | 0.003 | 0.025 | 0.019 | 90.0% | yes |

The cold-stratum stress check is important. Only 23 of 253 intervals are cold;
Snowbird has none. Modeled loss summed over the cold intervals is 0.0002 m at
Mica Creek, 0 at Niwot, and 0.0164 m at Paradise. Almost all of the detected
excess occurs in warm or mixed dry intervals. The evidence therefore points
toward melt timing or magnitude rather than an obvious cold-pack leak.

All 253 retained intervals have exactly zero fixture and guarded-gauge
precipitation, and reconstructed modeled net storage decline equals gross pack
loss. The dry result is therefore not an artifact of gross-loss accounting
during retained rain or snowfall.

Observed interval losses are also coarsely resolved. WTEQ loss is exactly zero
in 73.9% of Mica Creek intervals, 65.8% at Niwot, 74.6% at Paradise, and 58.8%
at Snowbird, consistent with the 0.1-inch pillow reporting increment. The dry
screen is useful for ranking evidence families, but its magnitude is not a
calibration target and cannot by itself validate a specific CoE equation.
Snowbird supplies the third passing site with exactly the minimum 10 annuals
(17 intervals), so the cohort verdict sits on the frozen coverage boundary and
should be described as threshold-passing but coverage-fragile rather than broad
all-winter confirmation. Its dry-year coverage is 10 of 35 primary years,
compared with 18 of 34 at Mica Creek, 34 of 44 at Niwot, and 29 of 41 at
Paradise.

The signal survives a deliberately conservative observation-resolution stress
check that adds one full 2.54 mm hidden observed-loss increment to every
interval. Niwot, Paradise, and Snowbird still pass with median annual
modeled-minus-observed loss of 0.0273, 0.0814, and 0.0123 m and positive-year
fractions of 0.853, 0.897, and 0.800. This supports the direction of the signal,
not exact melt magnitudes.

## Snowbird

Snowbird remains explicitly multifactor even though the cohort verdict is loss
priority:

- current fixture all-phase ceiling: 0.823 of observed peak;
- storage-effective modeled input: 0.714;
- modeled pack loss: 0.508;
- modeled storage on the observed peak date: 0.200; and
- within-window modeled peak: 0.382.

The station's PRCPSA ratio of 1.251 shows that a snow-adjusted station series
can restore mass headroom, but PRCPSA uses WTEQ changes and is circular against
the same pillow target. It cannot establish a forcing multiplier. Snowbird's
current-fixture input-feasibility signal and modeled pre-peak loss signal both
remain live. The former cannot distinguish forcing error from elevation,
footprint, redistribution, or target representativeness; neither signal can
absorb the entire discrepancy.

## Adjudication

The supported next target is a scale-aware audit of warm or mixed pre-peak loss
timing and energy attribution, preserving separate forcing, snowfall, storage,
and loss ledgers. Any prospective correction still requires independent,
scale-matched evidence. A future experiment should not fit the dry-interval
difference directly, should not use PRCPSA as independent precipitation truth,
and should retain Snowbird as a multifactor sentinel rather than treating it as
a clean loss-only case.
