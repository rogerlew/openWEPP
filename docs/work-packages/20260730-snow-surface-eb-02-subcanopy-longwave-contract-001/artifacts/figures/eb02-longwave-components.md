# Canopy cover shifts the snow longwave balance

These analytical curves show why canopy temperature and sky view must be
carried together. At zero cover every curve begins at the same atmospheric
longwave balance. As cover rises, the canopy occupies more of the radiative
hemisphere and the curves move toward the canopy's thermal emission.

In the example, above-canopy atmospheric longwave is held at
`270 W m^-2` and snow-surface temperature at `-5 degC`. A canopy colder than
the snow makes net longwave increasingly negative as cover rises; a warmer
canopy supplies increasingly positive net longwave. When canopy and snow have
the same temperature, canopy exchange cancels locally and only the shrinking
sky contribution remains.

The curves are not a forecast and do not represent a particular forest. They
isolate the contract equations so a reviewer can see the sign convention,
limiting behavior, and sensitivity to the unresolved canopy-temperature
provider. This is why EB-02 can settle the radiative equation while production
activation remains held for EB-03's coherent snow/canopy temperature decision.

Figure type: deterministic analytical illustration; no observed data and no

Accessibility: the horizontal axis is effective canopy cover from zero to one.
The vertical axis is net longwave toward snow, with zero marked by a dark
dashed line. Blue, purple, and orange curves represent canopy temperatures
five degrees colder than, equal to, and five degrees warmer than the snow,
