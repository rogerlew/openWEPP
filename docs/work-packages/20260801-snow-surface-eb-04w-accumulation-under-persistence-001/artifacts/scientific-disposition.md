# Scientific Disposition

Evidence mode: **Ran + Inference**.

## Decision

`DIAGNOSTIC_COMPLETE / CALIBRATION_HOLD / NO_PROMOTION`

## What The Evidence Establishes

Baseline seasonal modeled-peak magnitudes are about `0.39-0.62` of observed
peaks. Independently, baseline modeled SWE retained on the observed SWE-peak
dates has lane medians of about `0.21-0.46` of observed SWE, establishing a
larger observed-date storage deficit. At Paradise, initial SWE plus realized
snowfall SWE and retained rain remain below the observed peak in every
evaluated water year and cell.
Because retained rain depends on pack state and capacity, this localizes an
input-pathway/pre-peak-loss boundary but does not prove an external forcing
defect. Mica Creek, Niwot, and Snowbird likewise retain mixed ownership between
realized modeled input and pre-peak modeled melt/vapor/storage loss.

The new ledger is trustworthy for that classification: 16/16 cells completed;
phase, accumulation, and component closures are far inside `1e-12 m`; existing
WAT and common trace fields are behavior-neutral; and modeled redistribution is
explicitly zero because the process is not implemented.

## What The Evidence Does Not Establish

The result does not identify precipitation representativeness, gauge undercatch,
rain/snow phase, liquid retention, physical wind redistribution, or a pre-peak
loss process as the unique cause. It does not estimate a correction, support
site-residual fitting, validate the four CoE terms as pure energy fluxes,
demonstrate component efficacy, or authorize longwave/sublimation promotion.
Modeled-zero redistribution is not evidence of physical-zero redistribution.

## Successor Boundary

lanes require finer phase-conditioned timing and attribution across the realized
input pathway, liquid retention, and pre-peak losses before any process
amendment is admissible. Independent forcing/process observations would
strengthen that work but are not asserted as the sole resolution path. Continue
with EB-04X, whose paired Harvard hardwood/open design addresses density–depth
