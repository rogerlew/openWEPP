## Findings

### High

**A-1 — INV-ROUTE-018 mandates CREAMS-literal widening equations that the contract's own cited lineage and the mandated migrated runtime lanes contradict.**
Reference: `SC-ROUTE-001.md:78` (REF-ROUTE-CREAMS-CH3-WIDEN), `:140` (INV-ROUTE-018), `:636-647` (Widening Clock addendum section); evidence at `dcap.for:139-140, 227-228, 233` and `01_ws22_ws23_ws26_detachment.rs:329, 377`.
Issue: INV-ROUTE-018 and the addendum instruct computing the "widening rate basis (Eq. [I-136]-[I-138])" and advancing "per Eq. [I-133]-[I-135]." But the WEPP lineage **adapted** these equations, and the migrated WS18-WS31 lanes that INV-ROUTE-016 simultaneously mandates ("in their WS18-WS31 migrated lanes") implement the adapted forms: (a) the widening rate is **linear** in (tau_b − tau_cr) — `dwdti = excess * 2.0 * chnk * difsh / wtdsoi`, with the CREAMS `difsh**1.05` form literally present as a **commented-out line** in `dcap.for`; (b) the exponential law is the **modified** `wstar = (1 − exp(−1.0176·t*))/1.0176` (asymptote ≈ 0.983, never reaching W_f), not [I-133]'s `1 − exp(−t*)`; (c) the shear-distribution `f(x_b)` is a fitted log-polynomial (`ws22_shdist`), not the [I-137] closed form; (d) an `excess` multiplier appears that is absent from [I-136]. I verified all four points by reading both the pinned baseline and the current openWEPP Rust lane. The 1.35 factor of [I-128] is likewise dropped in the lineage detachment path (`adjsh = effsh` with `c adjsh = 1.35 * effsh` commented out) — the authority matrix's Row-6 fidelity note flagged exactly this for detachment ("cite CREAMS for provenance, not as an override") but the contract did not carry that discipline into the widening clock. `contract-disposition.md` records no adjudication of the widening form.
Impact: A hard-fail invariant currently specifies two mutually inconsistent operative laws (CREAMS-literal via the equation citations; WEPP-adapted via REF-ROUTE-GULLY-STATE and the WS18-WS31 mandate). An implementer following the equations as written breaks parity with the mandated machinery; an implementer reusing the lanes violates the equations as written. Either path "conforms" while the other fails — the invariant is not falsifiable as stated.
Disposition: Before ratification, add an explicit realization adjudication (one sentence in INV-ROUTE-018 plus a fidelity note in the addendum): either (i) the WEPP-adapted lineage forms (linear rate, 1.0176-modified exponential, fitted shear distribution) govern, with CREAMS [I-133]-[I-140] cited as provenance for the law's *structure* — the natural choice, consistent with the matrix's own fidelity note — or (ii) a deliberate CREAMS-literal override, with the baseline divergence recorded as a labeled decision. Also re-grade the INV-ROUTE-018 evidence tag (see A-4).

### Medium

**A-2 — The incision-to-widening transition interval is unspecified; the widening-clock text as written over-credits widening time.**
Reference: `SC-ROUTE-001.md:636-647` (Widening Clock: "advance Eq. [I-133]-[I-135] with `t - t_i = dtchr`"), `:613` (erosion-time operand row); lineage at `dcap.for` (`timpot = depmid*wtdsoi/di`, `timex = timsh − timpot`) and `01_ws22_ws23_ws26_detachment.rs:273`.
Issue: When a section reaches the nonerodible layer partway through an interval, the lineage (both Fortran and the migrated Rust lane) splits the erosion-time budget: incision consumes `timpot`, and only the residual `timex = timsh − timpot` drives widening. The contract's widening-clock section prescribes advancing by the full `dtchr` for any above-critical interval and never mentions the split; none of the nine test vectors (`:648-676`) covers mid-interval layer contact. Read literally, the contact interval double-counts erosion time (full incision through `depmid` plus full-`dtchr` widening), which also breaks the INV-ROUTE-019 geometry-mass consistency clause in that interval.
Impact: Spec ambiguity on a conservation-relevant seam; an implementation bypassing the lanes' internal `timpot/timex` logic would inflate detached mass at every layer-contact event.
Disposition: State explicitly that the interval's erosion-time budget is partitioned between incision-completion and widening within the contact interval (the `timpot`/`timex` lineage semantics with `dtchr` as the budget), and add a tenth test vector for mid-interval layer contact.

**A-3 — INV-ROUTE-019's storage-free closure identity makes INV-ROUTE-020(c)'s "suspended sediment mass associated with that storage" undefined as written.**
Reference: `SC-ROUTE-001.md:141` (INV-ROUTE-019), `:142` (INV-ROUTE-020(c)), `:693` (GAP-ROUTE-013).
Issue: The per-interval closure identity — ingress + detachment = egress + deposition, "No suspended sediment mass pool carries between intervals" — has no storage term; if it holds per interval, the suspended mass associated with end-of-grid routed water storage is identically zero by construction, and the deposit-at-grid-end rule disposes of a quantity the ratified solve form cannot produce. The rule is only meaningful if sediment egress is concentration-coupled to water egress (mass leaves only with water, so stored water retains mass) — but then the INV-ROUTE-019 identity needs a storage term for the final interval. The contract defines neither construction. (The water routing has storage; the quasi-steady sediment solve does not — the seam is real, and GAP-ROUTE-013's "ratified default" currently governs an uncomputable operand.)
Impact: The two hard-fail invariants are mutually inconsistent under one reading and vacuous under the other; test vector 6 (`:665-666`) cannot be built without an answer.
Disposition: Define the disposition mass constructively (e.g., per-interval egress mass = outlet flux × dtchr capped by concentration × exiting water volume, with the shortfall accumulating as the storage-associated suspended mass; or explicitly state the quasi-steady lane produces zero storage-associated mass and INV-ROUTE-020(c) guards only the recorded unsteady fallback lane). Amend GAP-ROUTE-013 to match.

**A-4 — Per-interval re-anchoring of the widening law is an inference, not a direct evaluation; the [DIRECT][Static] tag on INV-ROUTE-018 overstates.**
Reference: `SC-ROUTE-001.md:140` (INV-ROUTE-018 tag), `:78`; CREAMS `312-ch3.md:628-644`.
Issue: CREAMS defines `(dw/dt)_i` as the **initial** widening rate at nonerodible-layer contact and `t_i` as the contact time — [I-133]-[I-135] is a fixed-anchor interpolation for "the given Q." Re-anchoring every interval (fresh `W_f(Q_i)`, fresh rate at the current geometry, `W_i := W_current`) does **not** reproduce the single-anchor exponential trajectory even at constant Q (the re-linearized relaxation decays differently beyond first order). The scheme is nonetheless well-founded: the event-based lineage necessarily re-anchors per *event* with carried `werod` and each event's own Q (verified in `dcap.for`), so per-interval re-anchoring is the natural interval-ization of an existing lineage pattern — but that is an inference chain, not a direct reading. The authority matrix's "memoryless… native, not an invention" claim is slightly too strong for the same reason: the law is memoryless only given parameters that are themselves anchor-dependent.
Impact: Evidence-grading discipline (ADR-0017 posture): a refinement is being carried at DIRECT grade, unlike INV-ROUTE-016 which correctly labels its own refinement.
Disposition: Re-tag INV-ROUTE-018 as `[DIRECT][Static] + [INFERENCE][Static]` and add one clause labeling per-interval re-anchoring as the interval-ization of the lineage's per-event re-anchoring (refinement, with the same recorded-fallback posture as INV-ROUTE-016).
